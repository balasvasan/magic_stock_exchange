00The scenario

Magic Street Exchange (MSE) is the third-largest stock exchange in India, headquartered in Mumbai (BKC) with a technology center in Bangalore. Founded in 2009 by a consortium of mid-tier domestic brokerages, MSE has grown to ₹3,400 crore (~$410M USD) in annual revenue, a 920-person workforce, and a 14% market share in cash equities. Its niche is mid-cap and small-cap derivatives — about 280 underlying names where NSE and BSE underinvest, served by a self-built C++ matching engine called TARANG that emits an order-event firehose averaging 3.5 billion events per day and peaking at 9–12 billion on F&O expiry Thursdays. Roughly 24 million investor accounts trade on MSE through 380 trading member firms, with the retail tilt that defines the post-2020 Indian options boom: 78% of accounts are retail, contributing 45% of order volume and 60% of order count — small, frequent, retail orders that create exactly the noisy market microstructure manipulators love to exploit.

In Q2 2025 SEBI issued MSE a Show Cause Notice. The 2017-vintage vendor surveillance platform that MSE had been running was sized for 40,000 events per second; it was now drowning under 150,000 events per second on expiry Thursdays, silently shedding messages once its input queue exceeded ~2 million events. The Notice cited 14 specific manipulative episodes between January and March 2025 that the platform failed to detect — episodes the regulator caught in its own market replay and traced back to a single proprietary trading firm that was eventually disgorged ₹47 crore in unlawful gains. SEBI's inspection report named four root failures: alerts firing post-session ("of substantially diminished investigative value"), 92% of fired alerts closed by analysts as no-action ("operationally indistinguishable from no surveillance at all"), a 340-report STR documentation backlog with the oldest 70 days old, and "demonstrably inadequate" lineage and access controls on personal data of millions of investors. SEBI gave MSE 18 months to remediate or face restrictions on new product approvals. The DPDP Rules 2025 took effect in November 2025 in parallel, putting MSE in active enforcement scope under a separate regulator (the Data Protection Board) with penalties up to ₹250 crore for inadequate safeguards. Independent estimate of combined regulatory exposure: ₹150–300 crore worst case, plus ₹95 crore in already-foregone listing revenue from institutional clients citing "concerns about market integrity."

The board approved a ₹220 crore (~$26M) program — internally codenamed ARGUS, after the hundred-eyed watchman of Greek myth who never had every eye closed at once — to rebuild the entire surveillance stack on Cloudera Data Platform. Your job over the next ten days is to be the data engineering, ML engineering, and governance team that builds it. You will design and operate a streaming-ingest layer that absorbs the F&O firehose without falling behind, derive the cross-product temporal features that detect spoofing and layering and the Jane Street-style index manipulation patterns that defeated the legacy platform, train and deploy a supervised ML model that finally separates the 8% real signal from the 92% rules-engine noise, build a GenAI engine that drafts the SEBI-compliant Suspicious Transaction Reports analysts currently spend an hour each writing by hand, and prove DPDP Act 2023 compliance with cryptographic-grade Iceberg time-travel evidence that survives a Data Protection Board audit. Every deficiency closes.

01The five deficiencies

Each deficiency closes via specific checkpoints across specific modules. Failure to close all five is a SEBI / DPDP enforcement risk.

ARG-1
Peak F&O volume crisis

Legacy CEP engine sized for 40K events/sec; chokes at the 150K/sec expiry-Thursday peak — and even when it does fire, post-close detection latency makes intervention impossible. Closed by Module 1 (CDF + PyFlink CEP + SQL Stream Builder — three streaming engines, one Kafka backbone, sub-second pattern detection).

ARG-2
Temporal feature gap

No cross-product features; cannot detect Jane Street pattern (cash + futures + options correlated manipulation). Closed by Modules 2–3 (CDE / Spark on Iceberg).

ARG-3
92% false-positive flood

Analysts buried in noise; real cases buried with them. Closed by Module 5 (CML / XGBoost alert risk-ranking with manual promotion gates).

ARG-4
STR documentation backlog

40-minute hand-written narratives, 340-report backlog, oldest 70 days old. Closed by Module 6 (CAII / GenAI RAG engine — locally hosted).

ARG-5
No lineage / consent / erasure

DPDP §6(4) consent withdrawal unenforceable; DPDP §12 erasure unprovable. Closed by Modules 4 + 7 (CDW governed views, SDX Atlas + Ranger).

Four layers, top-down: Govern (SDX), Serve (CDW + CML + CAII + CDV), Process (CDE on Iceberg with MLflow + Airflow), Ingest (CDF/NiFi consuming 9 production + 2 DLQ Kafka topics). Govern cuts horizontally across all three lower layers.

⚠ CP-19 · COMPLIANCE GATE

DPDP §12 erasure provable via Iceberg time-travel

Cases 20–22 are erased through the workflow at src/governance/gdpr_erasure_workflow.py. The pre-erasure Iceberg snapshot ID is captured in argus_${STUDENT_ID}_gold.consent_audit (which has history.expire.enabled=false, so its history never expires). A post-erasure FOR SYSTEM_VERSION AS OF <pre_snap> query must return the erased investor's row; the same query at current snapshot must return zero rows; the audit row in consent_audit must persist. All three conditions for all three planted cases. Failure on any of them = capstone fail. See labs/lab-7-3-erasure.md.
