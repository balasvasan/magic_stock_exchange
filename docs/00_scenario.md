# Scenario — Magic Street Exchange

**Magic Street Exchange (MSE)** is the third-largest stock exchange in India, headquartered in Mumbai (BKC) with a technology center in Bangalore. Founded in 2009 by a consortium of mid-tier domestic brokerages, MSE has grown to ₹3,400 crore (~$410M USD) in annual revenue, a 920-person workforce, and a 14% market share in cash equities. Its niche is mid-cap and small-cap derivatives — about 280 underlying names where NSE and BSE underinvest, served by a self-built C++ matching engine called TARANG that emits an order-event firehose averaging 3.5 billion events per day and peaking at 9–12 billion on F&O expiry Thursdays. Roughly 24 million investor accounts trade on MSE through 380 trading member firms, with the retail tilt that defines the post-2020 Indian options boom: 78% of accounts are retail, contributing 45% of order *volume* and 60% of order *count* — small, frequent, retail orders that create exactly the noisy market microstructure manipulators love to exploit.

In Q2 2025 SEBI issued MSE a Show Cause Notice. The 2017-vintage vendor surveillance platform that MSE had been running was sized for 40,000 events per second; it was now drowning under 150,000 events per second on expiry Thursdays, silently shedding messages once its input queue exceeded ~2 million events. The Notice cited 14 specific manipulative episodes between January and March 2025 that the platform failed to detect — episodes the regulator caught in its own market replay and traced back to a single proprietary trading firm that was eventually disgorged ₹47 crore in unlawful gains. SEBI's inspection report named four root failures: alerts firing post-session ("of substantially diminished investigative value"), 92% of fired alerts closed by analysts as no-action ("operationally indistinguishable from no surveillance at all"), a 340-report STR documentation backlog with the oldest 70 days old, and "demonstrably inadequate" lineage and access controls on personal data of millions of investors. SEBI gave MSE 18 months to remediate or face restrictions on new product approvals. The DPDP Rules 2025 took effect in November 2025 in parallel, putting MSE in active enforcement scope under a separate regulator (the Data Protection Board) with penalties up to ₹250 crore for inadequate safeguards. Independent estimate of combined regulatory exposure: ₹150–300 crore worst case, plus ₹95 crore in already-foregone listing revenue from institutional clients citing "concerns about market integrity."

The board approved a ₹220 crore (~$26M) program — internally codenamed **ARGUS**, after the hundred-eyed watchman of Greek myth who never had every eye closed at once — to rebuild the entire surveillance stack on Cloudera Data Platform. Your job over the next ten days is to be the data engineering, ML engineering, and governance team that builds it. You will design and operate a streaming-ingest layer that absorbs the F&O firehose without falling behind, derive the cross-product temporal features that detect spoofing and layering and the Jane Street-style index manipulation patterns that defeated the legacy platform, train and deploy a supervised ML model that finally separates the 8% real signal from the 92% rules-engine noise, build a GenAI engine that drafts the SEBI-compliant Suspicious Transaction Reports analysts currently spend an hour each writing by hand, and prove DPDP Act 2023 compliance with cryptographic-grade Iceberg time-travel evidence that survives a Data Protection Board audit. Every deficiency closes. The mission is to leave Magic Street Exchange in a position where the next SEBI inspection finds nothing — and where the platform you've built becomes the reference architecture for surveillance modernization across Indian exchanges, broker-dealers, and the fast-growing GIFT City IFSC venues.
